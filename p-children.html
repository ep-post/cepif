Program Pillar 02

📚 Children of Migrant Workers

A parent's migration should never interrupt a child's education, safety, or future. We work to ensure continuity of learning and wellbeing for children in migrant households.

Focus Areas

What we do in this pillar

Education Continuity

Addressing the disruption to schooling caused by parental migration or family relocation — through school enrollment support, transfer facilitation, and community monitoring.

Learning Support

Bridge courses, remedial learning, and structured after-school support for children who have experienced gaps in their education due to migration.

Child Protection

Strengthening community-based child protection systems in high-migration source areas — where children are at elevated risk when caregivers are absent.

Digital Learning

Expanding access to quality digital learning tools for children in low-connectivity, high-migration communities — using low-bandwidth and offline-capable platforms.

The Problem We Address

Why this work is urgent

When parents migrate for work — seasonally or permanently — children are often left behind in source communities with grandparents or older siblings. The absence of a primary caregiver, combined with economic stress and disrupted routines, increases dropout risk, vulnerability to child labour, and early marriage for girls.

When families migrate together, children face the challenges of mid-year school transfer, language barriers, and unfamiliar environments. Both scenarios result in learning loss that compounds over time — with long-term consequences for economic mobility in the next generation.
